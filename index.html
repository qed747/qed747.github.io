Bayesian framework

The model starts with a prior probability by transaction archetype, converts it to odds, then multiplies by feature likelihood ratios derived from the normalized enforcement-action workbook. Smoothing keeps rare features from overpowering the estimate. 

posterior_odds = prior_odds x LR(country) x LR(product) x LR(route) x LR(red_flags) x policy_modifiers
posterior_probability = posterior_odds / (1 + posterior_odds)

Why Bayesian analysis

Sanctions and export-control risk is a classic evidence-updating problem. A proposed transaction starts with a baseline risk based on its general type. Each fact then changes the odds: destination, intermediary route, product, restricted-party nexus, licensing posture, military/end-use concerns, voluntary disclosure, remediation, and control weaknesses. Bayesian analysis is appropriate because it lets the tool combine incomplete but meaningful evidence without pretending that any single factor is determinative.

How the model works

The app estimates likelihood ratios from the loaded enforcement universe by asking whether a feature appears more often in higher-severity matters than in lower-severity matters. Higher-severity currently means a criminal posture or a listed penalty of at least $1 million. The model then multiplies those likelihood ratios with policy modifiers based on enforcement guidance, such as aggravation for restricted-party involvement or mitigation for voluntary self-disclosure and remediation.

The output is an enforcement-pattern score: a measure of similarity to public cases that agencies have charged, settled, prosecuted, or penalized. It is most useful for triage, escalation, and documenting why a transaction does or does not resemble prior enforcement matters.

Interpretation guardrails

The score is not a legal conclusion and not a true violation rate. In plain English, it asks: how much does this proposed transaction look like public sanctions and export-control matters that agencies have penalized, charged, or prosecuted?

Risk factor sources

Risk factors are analyst-coded from public agency enforcement frameworks and case documents, not from a single government data field. The tags reflect recurring factors in OFAC enforcement guidelines, BIS enforcement and red-flag materials, DOJ National Security Division voluntary-disclosure policy, DDTC consent agreements, and the linked public settlement, charging, plea, sentencing, or penalty records.

Pattern analysis

The Pattern Analysis tab compares normalized workbook records by countries, products, disclosure posture, agencies, enforcement posture, risk-factor tags, and recurring feature combinations. Its penalty summaries are descriptive associations, not causal estimates.
